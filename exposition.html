Math Exposition

Below, you can find a list of expository notes I have written over the years. Many of the notes are written for PROMYS minicourses (hour long talks for students) and counselor seminars (longer sequences of more advanced talks for counselors).

  • Geometric Number Theory (PROMYS 24) A sketch of the proof of unique factorization of ideals in Dedekind domains, the first central theorem in Algebraic Number Theory, from a(n algebro) geometric perspective.
  • 3264 Conics (PROMYS 24) A more-or-less complete explanation for how to obtain the count of there being 3264 conics tangent to 5 given conics in a plane. Dicusses relevant topics like Bezout's theorem and blowups. Plenty of diagrams.
  • Continued Fractions via Hyperbolic Geometry (Tshirt Talk PROMYS 24) One of the cornerstone topics of PROMYS is continued fractions, and in these notes, we explained how to obtain a lot of the results exploiting hyperbolic geometry. The talk accompanied the 2024 Tshirt design which computed the continued fraction of sqrt(6). (Try exercise 3.2!)
  • de Rham Cohomology (PROMYS 22 & 24) These are introductory notes on de Rham Cohomology, roughly covering 2/3 of the first chapter of Bott & Tu, with some extra details and examples of my own. PROMYS '22 was the first time I looked into this topic, and it has since become one of my favorite pieces of mathematics.
  • An Introduction to Hyperbolic Geometry (PROMYS 24) These are notes intended to be an introduction to hyperbolic geometry. Most of the exposition is done synthetically, with material borrowed from Thurston's book on 3 dimensional geometry. An updated version with more diagrams will be made... eventually.
  • Weierstrass ℘ Function (PROMYS 22) This is an exposition of the Weierstrass ℘ function which parametrizes Elliptic curves over the complex numbers.
  • Lie Groups in Differential Equations (PROMYS 22) These are notes on using Lie groups to study and exploit the symmetries of differential equations.
  • Projective Geometry (PROMYS 22) These notes are an introduction to projective geometry (more precisely the projective plane). The main application presented is using the ideas of projective geometry to find the equation of a curve given an envelope of lines.
  • Group Cohomology Paper These are some notes on group cohomology motivated by computing the cohomology of K(G,1) spaces, as presented in Brown's book. It was written as a final project for an algebraic topology class.
  • Fractional Linear Transformations (Expo Lab, PROMYS 20) This was the result of an "exploration lab" at PROMYS, which are group projects to explore known topics as baby research projects for first year students. The premise was to explore fractional linear transformations of finite fields. Some of the results include finding the structure of these maps, compute all conjugacy classes of maps, and an interesting application to quadratic reciprocity for -1 and -3.
  • Prime Number Theorem (PRIMES 20) These were notes written after a group reading project on the prime number theorem at MIT PRIMES. A proof is presented with the requisite complex analysis background along with some exposition on p-adic numbers and the Adelic viewpoint of the Zeta function.
